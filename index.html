On stage at CECU 2026

The AI Enrollment Stack: 2026 benchmarks, signals, and the end of last-click thinking.

Tuesday, June 23 3:30–4:30 PM ET Room 6

This session is about what's actually changed in career college enrollment marketing over the past year and what programs should be doing differently because of it. Google's AI Max and Performance Max have restructured how paid search campaigns are built and how results get attributed. Meta's Andromeda rewrite has changed how creative and audience targeting interact at the platform level. Both of those shifts have moved enrollment inquiry generation further away from the last-click attribution model most programs use to report results.

Brad Stephenson and Josh Muskin from Level Agency walk through what these changes mean in practice, grounded in Level's 2026 Higher Education Performance Benchmarks data. Christopher Mercado, Director of Marketing, US Operations at Northbridge University, brings the institutional perspective: what these platform and measurement shifts look like when you're the person responsible for hitting enrollment targets.

Attendees will leave with:

  • A framework for restructuring campaigns for AI Max, Performance Max, and Meta Andromeda.
  • A value-based bidding approach and differentiated conversion signal strategy.
  • Specific adjustments for AI-driven search visibility.
  • A path from last-click toward incrementality-based measurement.
  • A clearer picture of how prospective student research behavior has shifted and how messaging should follow.

The 2026 Higher Education Performance Benchmarks Report

Benchmarks built from the vertical you actually operate in.

Level Agency produces this report annually in partnership with LeadSquared, built from campaign data across $100M+ in career college program ad spend. The 2026 edition covers cost per inquiry and cost per enrolled student across program types and channels, how AI-driven ad delivery is changing campaign performance and what it means for measurement, where lead quality is improving and where it requires more scrutiny, and what the data shows about inquiry response time and nurture sequences as factors in enrollment conversion.

The report is built from actual campaign data, not surveys or estimates extrapolated from general higher ed research. If your team is evaluating your current cost per enrolled student against the market, or making the case for a budget or channel mix decision, this gives you a benchmark built from the same vertical you're operating in.

Brad and Josh draw directly from this data in their Tuesday session. Download it before CECU and bring your questions to the floor. Physical copies available at Booth 611.
